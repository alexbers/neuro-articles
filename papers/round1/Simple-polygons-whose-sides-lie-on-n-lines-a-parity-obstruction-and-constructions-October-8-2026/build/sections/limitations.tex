\section{What is not verified; computational checks; open questions}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Not formally verified.}
The following are proved on paper only.
\begin{itemize}
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:forced}, including the reduction from line arrangements to wiring diagrams.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:percycle}, the sweep argument.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:A}, the potential-function computation.
\item Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}, Corollary~\ref{cor:main}, and the upper bounds $M(7)\le18$ and $M(9)\le33$.
\end{itemize}
For each fixed $n$, the one-step identity for $\Phi$ in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:A} is a finite
statement about the $n!$ sweep states, and Lemma~\ref{lem:percycle} is a finite statement about the
commutation classes of reduced words of $w_0$. Both are natural targets for a kernel-checked Lean proof,
but we have not produced one. An earlier attempt to certify Lemma~\ref{lem:A} for $n=7$ in Lean, by a
kernel-evaluated breadth-first search over the sweep states of all $5040$ permutations, ran out of kernel
memory; we chose not to fall back on \texttt{native\_decide}.

\paragraph{Computational checks.} These are evidence, not proofs. The scripts are in the directory
\texttt{scripts/} next to this paper; each was run on October 8, 2026.
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{enumerate\_classes.c} enumerates every commutation class of reduced words of $w_0$, i.e.\
  every simple arrangement of $n$ pseudolines as a wiring diagram with numbered wires, by generating the
  lexicographically least word of each class. For each class it builds $G$ from the order of the crossings
  along each wire, counts the components, and counts the $A$-crossings on each cycle. For $n=5,7,9$ it
  visits all $62$, $24\,698$ and $112\,018\,190$ classes; these counts agree with OEIS
  \href{https://oeis.org/A006245}{A006245}~\cite{A006245}. In every class, $\#A\equiv r(r+1)/2\pmod 2$
  and every cycle of $G$ contains an odd number of $A$-crossings. The numbers of components are
  distributed as follows.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rl@{}}
\toprule
$n$ & components of $G$ (number of classes)\\
\midrule
$3$ & $1$ ($2$)\\
$5$ & $1$ ($22$),\ $3$ ($40$)\\
$7$ & $2$ ($7\,870$),\ $4$ ($10\,220$),\ $6$ ($6\,608$)\\
$9$ & $2$ ($6\,360\,134$),\ $4$ ($35\,038\,062$),\ $6$ ($39\,168\,090$),\ $8$ ($23\,868\,000$),\ $10$ ($7\,583\,904$)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
  In particular $G$ is never a single cycle for $n=7$ or $n=9$. Since every simple arrangement of lines
  gives a reduced word of $w_0$, this confirms $M(7)\le18$ and $M(9)\le33$ independently of
  \cref{sec:parity}, though it still relies on Proposition~\ref{prop:forced}. The pure Python version
  \texttt{enumerate\_classes.py} does the same for $n\le7$.
\item \texttt{potential\_check.py} verifies the one-step identity for $\Phi$ in the proof of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:A} for every permutation and every ascent, for $n\le 8$.
\item \texttt{parity\_random.py} checks the parity of the number of components of $G$ and the per-cycle
  statement of Lemma~\ref{lem:percycle} on random reduced words of $w_0$, for every odd $n\le23$
  ($3000$ words for $n\le13$, $500$ words for $15\le n\le23$). No violation occurred. The smallest numbers
  of components seen were $6,8,9,9,12$ for $n=15,17,19,21,23$.
\item \texttt{verify\_constructions.py} rechecks the four polygons of \cref{sec:constructions} with exact
  rational arithmetic, independently of Lean: the vertices in \texttt{lean/Main.lean} are the intersection
  points named in \texttt{data/polyN.json}, the polygons are simple in the sense of \cref{sec:reduction},
  the arrangements are in general position, the polygons for $n=11,13$ coincide with $G$, and the
  polygons for $n=7,9$ use all intersection points except those of one triangle.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open questions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is $M(n)=\binom n2$ for every $n\equiv\pm3\pmod 8$? We confirmed this for $n=3,5,11,13$. Brief
  simulated-annealing searches for $n=19$ and $n=21$ (four runs of $10^6$ steps each) reached $3$
  components but not $1$.
\item Is $M(n)=\binom n2-3$ for every $n\equiv\pm1\pmod 8$? We confirmed this for $n=7$ and $n=9$. The
  extremal polygons of Proposition~\ref{prop:forced}(2) use all pairs of lines except one triangle; it is
  not clear that such a polygon exists for every $n\equiv\pm1\pmod 8$.
\item What is $M(n)$ for even $n$? The closed-trail bound is $n(n-2)/2$, and DSM's $24$-gon on $8$
  lines attains it for $n=8$. We have not studied even $n$; note that Definition~\ref{def:G} needs $n-1$
  to be even.
\item Which numbers of components does $G$ have, for a given $n$, beyond the parity constraint? By the
  table above the minimum is $1$ for $n=3,5$ and $2$ for $n=7,9$; by \cref{sec:constructions} it is $1$
  for $n=11,13$. Is the minimum always $1$ or $2$ according to the parity?
\end{enumerate}
